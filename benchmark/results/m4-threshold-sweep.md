## Chosen operating point

**threshold = 0.94**, measured hit rate 14.5%, measured false-hit rate 8.0% with the entity guard active.

Justification: among the candidate thresholds that keep the cache meaningfully useful (hit rate >= 10%, a floor only to exclude degenerate near-zero-hit-rate points), this is the one that minimises the guarded false-hit rate - the Pareto-best point actually measured, not a guess. It does not yet clear AC3 (hit rate >= 35%) or AC4 (false-hit rate <= 5%) simultaneously; per this phase's plan, that is acceptable as long as the numbers are measured and the choice is justified, with further tuning left to Phase 09's full experiment suite.

**Residual false-hit analysis.** At every threshold swept, the guarded false-hit rate is strictly greater than zero. Inspecting the corpus explains why: the entity/numeric guard (EntityNumericGuard) only ever compares extracted numbers, named entities, and dates, exactly as F4's correctness note specifies - it has no concept of negation or polarity. Several adversarial pairs in the corpus change meaning through negation or a direction flip alone ("is open" vs "is closed", "rose" vs "fell", "increased" vs "decreased", "expected" vs "not expected") with no number, entity, or date changing at all; these are, by the guard's defined scope, structurally undetectable by it and will pass through as a hit whenever their embedding similarity clears the threshold. This is a genuine, documented limitation, not a bug - closing it would mean extending the guard beyond what F4 specifies (e.g. a small negation-word check), which is a reasonable Phase 09 follow-up, not required for this phase's exit criterion.
